# Data Sources

CaribFlow is grounded in public and institutionally governed data families relevant to trade,
logistics, and corridor activation.

## Public source families

- UN Comtrade
- LeKiosque / DGDDI (French customs)
- FAOSTAT
- World Bank WITS / TRAINS
- World Bank Logistics Performance Index
- UNCTAD Liner Shipping Connectivity Index
- ePing SPS/TBT notifications

### Why French customs sits in this list

UN Comtrade merges the French overseas departments into France. A corridor whose origin is one of
those territories therefore has no supply side in Comtrade at all — not a small figure, not a zero,
simply no reporter.

For those origins the supply side is read from French customs (LeKiosque / DGDDI) or it is not read
at all. That is a boundary of the data, not a preference between providers, and it is the reason a
territory can be fully covered on the demand side and absent on the supply side.

## Institution-specific sources

- CARICOM Secretariat (official membership records)
- EUR-Lex (treaty and regulation texts)
- national customs or trade publications
- corridor-level practitioner feedback where properly anonymized
- licensed or governed institutional records where available

## How the sources are used

The sources support different parts of the corridor brief:

- trade magnitude
- logistics context
- connectivity
- tariff and policy context
- evidence for activation barriers

The important point is that the system is evidence-led.
It does not invent a corridor condition when the source is absent.

## What is not republished here

This public repo does not republish bulk raw provider data.
It references source families and demonstrates the logic of use.

## Licensing and attribution posture

Some sources are public but still require attribution.
Some are public but bounded by terms that should not be ignored.
Some are institutional and should remain anonymized or summarized.

The rule is to state the source family clearly, preserve attribution where required, and avoid
turning the public repository into a mirror of the provider's own data product.
